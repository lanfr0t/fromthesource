The Detour That Made the Trip

A tornado warning, a college visit, and one sandwich shop in Hillsdale, Michigan that earned a return trip

✦

The menu at Handmade — Hillsdale Street, Hillsdale, Michigan. The Signature Sandies board is top right. You want the Handie Sandie.

Restaurant  Handmade Sandwiches & Beverages Location  Hillsdale, Michigan Type  Mom & Pop Discovery Verdict  Go Back Soon

We didn't come to Hillsdale, Michigan for the food. We came because our daughter is going to be playing basketball at Hillsdale College in 2027, and we wanted to see the place — the campus, the town, the life she'll be stepping into. What we didn't expect was to leave already planning a return trip for the sandwich alone.

The visit started with a 1:10 AM wake-up call. Tornado sirens. We jumped out of bed in our Airbnb, pulled up the weather channel, and watched a tornado track a few miles south of town until around 2 AM before we could settle back down. Not exactly the relaxed college visit we'd imagined. By morning, we needed coffee, food, and something to restore our faith in the day.

Handmade delivered all three.

"There are places you walk into and know immediately that someone cared — about the space, the menu, and the people coming through the door. Handmade is one of those places."

The Place

What Handmade Actually Is

Handmade Sandwiches & Beverages sits on Hillsdale Street in a building that was renovated from the ground up by its owner. The space is clean and modern with a small-town warmth that a lot of newer restaurants spend a lot of money trying to fake and usually can't quite pull off. The ordering setup is straightforward — you place your order at the front, make your way to the back to pay, and pass the cookie case on the way. Consider yourself warned about the cookie case.

The menu breaks down into Create Your Own, Signature Sandies, and Shareables — plus soups, hand-dipped ice cream, and coffee. Everything is made to order. The meat is hand-sliced. The bread is fresh. These aren't marketing claims — you can taste the difference.

The vibe is exactly what a great small-town lunch spot should be: a mix of college students, local regulars, and people like us who wandered in and immediately felt at home. Someone behind the counter called a regular by name before they'd finished walking through the door.

What We Ordered

The Handie Sandie — A Case Study

Signature Sandie
The Handie Sandie
Ham · Turkey · Bacon · Horseradish Cheddar · Herb Mayo

This sandwich is better than it has any right to be. On paper it's a classic deli combination — but the execution is what separates it. The horseradish in the cheddar provides a slow, building heat that doesn't overwhelm so much as it keeps you honest. The herb mayo is doing serious work: bright, fresh, and aromatic in a way that lifts every bite. Fresh ingredients throughout — you can taste that nothing came out of a bag. The balance of spice, richness, and herb is the kind of thing that seems simple until you try to replicate it at home and realize how carefully it was calibrated.

One note from a fellow Tripadvisor traveler worth echoing: there are no prices on the wall menu. The sandwich runs around $17 for a 14-inch, which caught at least one visitor off guard. It's on the higher end for a small Michigan town, but in the context of the quality and portion size, it's fair. Just know going in.

The Story Behind It

A Family That Builds Things

Owner Spotlight
Derek Spiteri

Derek Spiteri opened Handmade after spending a year renovating the old Oakley building on Hillsdale Street. He's a Hillsdale native — son of John Spiteri, who has run Checker Records in town for decades — and part of a family with four generations of small business ownership in the same community. A great-grandfather's cash register from an old shoe store sits behind the counter at Handmade as a reminder of where it all started.

Derek's vision was a place that served both the college community and the town — a quality lunch stop with craft beverages and a menu built around great ingredients rather than shortcuts. That vision has held. Handmade was voted one of the top 10 sub shops in Michigan by MLive, has weathered a pandemic without cutting staff, and has become one of those rare places where both students and longtime locals feel like regulars. "It's a nice mix of the college and town communities coming together," one Hillsdale professor told the college newspaper. "Derek is so passionate about his sandwiches."

There's something worth pausing on here. Hillsdale is a small town, and Derek Spiteri didn't have to build something this good. He could have done something adequate and been fine. Instead he spent a year renovating a building, hand-slices the meat, sources quality ingredients, and clearly still shows up every day caring about the result. That's not nothing. That's the whole thing, actually.

The Verdict

We came to Hillsdale to see a college campus. We're going back for the sandwich. That's the highest compliment a small-town lunch spot can earn from a one-time visitor — the immediate desire to be a regular.

Why This Matters

The Case for Eating Local

One of the things we're chasing with these posts is the experience you can't replicate at a chain, a hotel restaurant, or anywhere that optimizes for consistency over character. Handmade is the argument for always asking a local where to eat instead of opening a national review app.

Derek Spiteri built something that belongs to Hillsdale. The great-grandfather's cash register behind the counter isn't a prop — it's a statement about continuity, about showing up for a community across generations. You taste that kind of intention in the food, even if you couldn't quite articulate why.

We hope our daughter eats there every week.

Next visit: back to Hillsdale when she commits — and we'll be trying the rest of the menu.
